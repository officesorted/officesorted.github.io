The £1,000 Trading Allowance: Do You Need to Tell HMRC About Your Side Hustle?

Updated 16 November 2026

The trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 a year (gross, before expenses) from self-employment or casual work without paying tax on it or telling HMRC, in most cases.

If your income is under £1,000

You usually don't need to register for Self Assessment for that income. Keep simple records anyway, in case your income grows or HMRC asks.

If your income is over £1,000

You'll usually need to register for Self Assessment (by 5 October after the end of the tax year) and file a return. You then choose either:

  • Claim your actual expenses, or
  • Deduct the £1,000 allowance instead of expenses.

Rule of thumb: if your allowable expenses are under £1,000, claiming the allowance gives a lower taxable profit.

Things to watch

  • Selling your own unwanted items isn't trading; buying to resell is.
  • Platforms like Etsy, eBay and Vinted now report sellers' income to HMRC above certain levels, so keep records.
  • You can't use the allowance against income from a business partnership, or (generally) income from your employer.
