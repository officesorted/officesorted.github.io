Council Tax: Pay Over 10 or 12 Months? (UK Guide)

Updated 16 November 2026

Most councils in England and Wales collect council tax in 10 monthly instalments, usually April to January, leaving February and March "free". That makes 10 months a bit tighter and two months a bit looser, which can catch people out.

Can I pay over 12 months?

In England and Wales you can ask your council to spread it over 12 monthly instalments instead. Most councils let you do this online or by phone; it's best to ask before the start of the tax year in April. Scottish councils also offer different payment schedules, so check your council's website.

10 vs 12: which is better?

  • 12 instalments make monthly budgeting smoother. The same amount every month.
  • 10 instalments give you two council-tax-free months, handy if February and March are usually tight (after Christmas, before spring bills).

There's no difference in the total you pay.

How to budget it either way

Whichever you choose, budget the yearly bill ÷ 12 every month. If you pay over 10 months, put the difference aside in Feb and March, or treat those months as your buffer. Example: a £1,720 bill is £172 over 10 months, but £143.33 a month in your budget.

Check you're not paying too much

  • Single person discount: if you're the only adult in your home you can usually get 25% off.
  • Disregards: some people (for example full-time students) aren't counted, which can mean a discount.
  • Council Tax Reduction: on a low income or benefits? Apply to your council.
  • Your band: you can check it on GOV.UK and compare similar homes on your street.
