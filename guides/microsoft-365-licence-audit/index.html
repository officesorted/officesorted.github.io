How to Find Unused Microsoft 365 Licences (Small Business Guide)

Updated 16 November 2026

In most small businesses, Microsoft 365 is the biggest software bill, and it's easy to pay for licences nobody uses. The usual causes: leavers whose accounts were never cleaned up, licences bought "just in case", and people on a bigger plan than they need.

1. Compare purchased vs assigned

In the Microsoft 365 admin centre go to Billing → Licenses. For each product you'll see how many licences you've bought and how many are assigned. Any gap is money you're spending on nothing. Reduce the quantity at your next billing point (check your commitment terms first: annual subscriptions usually can't be reduced mid-term).

2. Find leavers still holding a licence

Go to Users → Active users and sort by name. Anyone who has left should have their mailbox handled (convert to a shared mailbox if you need to keep it; shared mailboxes under 50 GB don't need a licence) and their licence removed.

3. Find inactive users

Under Reports → Usage, look at active users over the last 90 days. Someone with no email, Teams or OneDrive activity probably doesn't need a paid licence.

4. Check the plan level

Not everyone needs Business Premium. If a user doesn't need the extra security and device management features, Business Standard or Basic may do. Mix plans per person rather than giving everyone the top tier.

5. Make it a leaver-checklist step

The fix that lasts: add "remove licence" to your leaver process, on their last day.
