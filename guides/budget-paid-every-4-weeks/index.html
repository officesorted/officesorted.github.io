How to Budget When You're Paid Every 4 Weeks (UK)

Updated 16 November 2026

If you're paid every four weeks, you don't get paid 12 times a year. You get paid 13 times: 52 weeks ÷ 4 = 13. That sounds like good news, and it is, but it also makes monthly budgeting confusing, because your rent, council tax and most bills are monthly.

The simple formula

Turn your 4-weekly pay into a true monthly figure:

Monthly income = 4-weekly pay × 13 ÷ 12

So £1,000 every 4 weeks is really £1,083.33 a month on average. The catch: in 11 months you'll only receive one pay packet. In one month (it moves each year) you'll get two.

Two ways to budget 4-weekly pay

1. Budget on one pay packet a month (easiest)

Plan every month as if you only get £1,000. Your bills are always covered, and the month with two paydays becomes a "bonus" month. Decide now what the 13th pay goes to: an emergency fund, Christmas, a debt, or next year's car insurance.

2. Budget on the true monthly average (more accurate)

Plan on £1,083.33 and move the difference into a savings pot each month so it's there when you need it. This suits people with tight budgets who need every pound working, but it needs a little discipline.

Watch out for

  • Direct debit dates: if a bill comes out the day before payday, move the date. Most companies let you choose.
  • Universal Credit: UC uses monthly assessment periods, so a month with two 4-weekly pay packets can reduce that month's payment. Check your UC journal and plan for it.
  • Fortnightly pay works the same way: 26 paydays a year, so two months a year have three paydays (×26÷12).

Quick checklist

  • Find your next 13 paydays and mark the double-pay month
  • Budget each month on one pay packet
  • Give the 13th pay a job before it arrives
  • Check bills don't leave before payday
